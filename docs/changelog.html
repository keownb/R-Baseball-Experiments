Changelog

What changed, and when.

Pipeline audit

October 30, 2026

A full review of how the charts are built, after the 2026 World Series failed to show up on the Brewers' charts. Everything below is corrected in the current renders.

What was wrong

  • 2026 postseason missing everywhere. The parallel render workers were handed the current reference tables and then reloaded the code, which reset them to the Lahman database (which ends in 2025). Workers therefore drew no 2026 postseason lines for any club and classified 2026 rookies without their positions. The FanGraphs charts were also captioned as Baseball-Reference for the same reason.
  • About 500 players never joined to Lahman's tables because Lahman and Baseball-Reference spell their ids differently (apostrophes, periods, suffixes). Johan Santana, CC Sabathia, Paul O'Neill, Rafael Furcal, Steve O'Neill, Pete O'Brien, J.D. Drew and others were missing from position charts, team breakdowns and award markers. Lahman ids are now reconciled to Baseball-Reference's.
  • 19th-century and Federal League clubs that reused a modern code were credited to the modern franchise (Al Spalding and Ross Barnes of the 1871–75 Red Stockings in the Red Sox top 5; the Baltimore Terrapins on the Orioles; Cy Young's Spiders years on Cleveland in the FanGraphs view). Franchise membership now comes from Lahman's team table for every season, so the 1901–02 Baltimore Orioles also follow Lahman's and Baseball-Reference's lineage into the Yankees.
  • Pre-1969 pennant winners had no postseason marker. A vertical line marks each season the club won at least one postseason series, styled by the biggest one it won (solid World Series, dashed pennant/LCS, dotted Division Series, dot-dash Wild Card). Before 1969 the pennant was decided by the regular season, so a club that won its league and then lost the World Series had no line at all; it now gets the dashed pennant line, the same as a later LCS winner.
  • Shohei Ohtani's pitching WAR was plotted on the DH panel of the Angels and Dodgers breakdowns. Pitching WAR now goes to the SP/RP panels for two-way players.
  • Z-score line widths skipped 2020. Qualification thresholds (60 games, 15 starts) were not scaled to the 60-game season, so most positions had no comparison pool and the 2019→2020 segment vanished from hundreds of lines. Thresholds now scale with the schedule.
  • Traded players in the newest season had all their games credited to their last club by the MLB Stats API query the pipeline used; it now queries each club separately.
  • All-Star markers for a player traded mid-season appear only on the club he represented, and "best in gap years" lines only connect consecutive seasons.

Process

  • One build script now refreshes the data, checks it (season coverage, every team code mapped, every regular has a position, postseason complete, awards present once announced), renders, checks the rendered files against the data (including parsing the SVGs to confirm the latest World Series line is actually drawn), and only then installs and deploys. Renders are deterministic, so unchanged data produces identical files.

2026 season update

October 29, 2026

Data

  • All charts now run through the end of the 2026 regular season. Postseason markers include the full 2026 bracket (Brewers over Rays in the World Series).
  • FanGraphs WAR is available as an alternate source on both pages (the "WAR Source" control). Pitcher rankings differ substantially between the two systems, since fWAR is FIP-based; see About.
  • Seasons newer than the Lahman database (currently 2026) are filled from the MLB Stats API, so All-Star markers, postseason lines, z-score comparison pools and rookies' positions no longer stop a year early. MVP and Cy Young markers for 2026 will appear after the November announcements.

Fixes

  • The Athletics' 2025 and 2026 seasons were missing: Baseball-Reference changed their team code to ATH and the franchise map didn't know it.
  • Postseason markers were attributed to the wrong franchises in several cases (the Royals' postseasons showed on the A's, the Mariners' on the Brewers; the New York Giants', Milwaukee Braves' and 1982 Brewers' pennants were missing). Team breakdowns for those eras also classified starters, relievers and DHs incorrectly for the same reason.
  • The x-axis was capped at 2026 instead of following the data.

Site

  • Charts are now vector (SVG): sharp at any zoom, smaller than the old PNGs, and text is selectable.
  • Moved to baseball.bricekeown.net. The old GitHub Pages address redirects here.

Initial release

March–April 2026

30-team grid explorer and per-team position breakdowns with era filters, z-score line widths, award markers and postseason indicators, built on Baseball-Reference WAR and the Lahman database.
